Rotary kilns are the core thermal processing unit in cement and mineral industries. The rotating cylindrical shell, lined with refractory bricks, carries material through a controlled temperature gradient from the feed end to the discharge end.

**Cement & Gypsum Applications**

For gypsum calcination, the kiln converts raw gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) to hemihydrate or anhydrite by controlled dehydration at temperatures between 120°C and 180°C for hemihydrate (plaster of Paris) or above 400°C for anhydrite. For cement, the kiln drives the clinkering reactions at approximately 1400–1450°C.

**Construction**

The shell is fabricated from structural steel plate, supported on riding rings and trunnion rollers. The refractory lining is selected based on the operating temperature and material being processed. Variable-speed drives allow throughput adjustment.
